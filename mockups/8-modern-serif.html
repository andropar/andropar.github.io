01 — About

Advancing neuroimaging efficiency

I'm pursuing a PhD in computational cognitive neuroscience at the Max Planck Institute for Human Cognition and Brain Science in Leipzig, under the supervision of Martin Hebart.

My research is centered on improving the efficiency of data acquisition in large-scale deep phenotyping studies within vision science. I explore two fundamental questions: whether we can record the human visual system at substantially higher temporal frequencies than currently believed feasible, and whether computational methods can intelligently pre-select visual stimuli to maximize information gained from each scanning session.

Academic Background

I obtained a Master's degree in Computer Science at Leipzig University, focusing on data science, machine learning, medical image processing, and neuroscience. My master's thesis, completed at the MPI CBS under Martin Hebart and Katja Seeliger, investigated methods for generating images that elicit high activations in specific cortical regions using deep learning models of the visual system.

02 — Research

Current Focus

Focus Area I
High-frequency fMRI
Exploring temporal limits of neuroimaging to capture rapid visual processing dynamics.
Focus Area II
Stimulus optimization
Developing computational frameworks for intelligent experimental stimulus selection.
Focus Area III
Deep phenotyping
Contributing to large-scale vision initiatives mapping human visual representations.

In my research, I integrate computational modeling and machine learning methods with functional neuroimaging, specifically fMRI, to address questions about optimizing both the temporal aspects of data collection and the strategic selection of visual stimuli for large-scale studies.
