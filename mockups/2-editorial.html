Introduction

I'm pursuing a PhD in computational cognitive neuroscience at the Max Planck Institute for Human Cognition and Brain Science in Leipzig, under the supervision of Martin Hebart.

My research focuses on making data acquisition for large-scale deep phenotyping studies in vision science more efficient. This involves exploring whether we can record the human visual system at much higher temporal frequencies than previously thought possible, and developing methods to pre-select the most informative images that maximize the insights gained from each scanning session.

Academic Background

I obtained a Master's degree in Computer Science at Leipzig University, with coursework spanning data science, machine learning, medical image processing, and neuroscience. My master's thesis, completed at the MPI CBS under Martin Hebart and Katja Seeliger, explored methods for generating images that elicit high activations in specific brain areas using deep learning models of the human visual system.

Research Focus

Making vision science more efficient

"Can we record the human visual system at frequencies far beyond what's currently believed possible?"

In my research, I integrate computational modeling and machine learning methods to address fundamental questions about neuroimaging efficiency. I'm particularly interested in how we can optimize both the temporal aspects of data collection and the strategic selection of visual stimuli.

Current Projects

My work centers on high-frequency fMRI acquisition and intelligent stimulus selection for the THINGS initiative—a large-scale effort to comprehensively map human visual representations across thousands of object categories.
