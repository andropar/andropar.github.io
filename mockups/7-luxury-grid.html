Introduction

Making vision science more efficient

I'm pursuing a PhD in computational cognitive neuroscience at the Max Planck Institute for Human Cognition and Brain Science in Leipzig, under the supervision of Martin Hebart.

My research focuses on enhancing the efficiency of data acquisition in large-scale deep phenotyping studies within vision science. At the core of my work are two fundamental questions: Can we record neural responses in the human visual system at temporal frequencies substantially higher than currently achievable? And can computational methods strategically pre-select visual stimuli to maximize information gained from each experimental session?

Academic Background

I obtained a Master's degree in Computer Science at Leipzig University, with coursework spanning data science, machine learning, medical image processing, and neuroscience. My master's thesis, completed at the MPI CBS under Martin Hebart and Katja Seeliger, explored methods for generating images that elicit high activations in specific brain areas using deep learning models of the human visual system.

Current Work

Research Focus

"Pushing the boundaries of temporal resolution in functional neuroimaging."
Focus I
High-temporal-resolution fMRI
Investigating the feasibility of dramatically increasing the sampling rate of functional neuroimaging to capture fine-grained temporal dynamics of visual processing beyond current conventions.
Focus II
Optimal stimulus selection
Developing computational frameworks for intelligent selection of experimental stimuli to maximize information gain in large-scale neuroimaging studies, making every scan count.
Focus III
Deep phenotyping initiatives
Contributing to comprehensive mapping efforts such as the THINGS initiative, characterizing human visual representations across thousands of object categories.
